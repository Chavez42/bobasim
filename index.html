Layer 01 — Cream · the pitch

Four rules the whole game answers to.

If a feature doesn't serve one of these, it doesn't ship. That's the entire scope control mechanism.

Pillar 01

The drink is an object

No "click to make drink." Every cup exists in world space, gets filled, layered, sealed and handed over. You should be able to look at a finished cup on the counter and read what's in it.

Pillar 02

Prep is the real game

Most shop sims put everything into the rush. This one puts it into opening. Under-brew the tea or under-cook the pearls and you'll feel it at 2pm, with no undo.

Pillar 03

Tactility over UI

Shaking, pouring, scooping, sealing and stamping each get a distinct signature. Eyes closed, you should still know which action just happened.

Pillar 04

Growth is spatial

Progress isn't a number going up. The footprint changes: cart, kiosk, storefront, second location. You walk through your own success.

One day, four phases

Roughly twenty real minutes.

Layer 02 — Milk · the drink system

A cup remembers everything that went into it.

A cup doesn't hold a recipe reference. It holds a record of every ingredient, in order, with volume, temperature and the freshness of the batch it came from. Hover a band.

Scoring

Graded against the order, not against perfection.

Same drink, different customer, different score. A connoisseur notices agitation and freshness. A kid with popping boba does not.

Agitation, three states

Watching the bands merge is the payoff. Going past the target ruins it.

Rendering note

The liquid is a clip-plane fake, not a fluid sim: four visible bands maximum, wobble driven from cup velocity, settled pearls baked to one instanced mesh. Real fluid would eat the schedule and no player would be able to read the cup anyway.

Layer 03 — Tea · the prep system

Every prep item has a lead time and a lifetime.

Prep time is fixed. Over-prep and you bin money at close. Under-prep and you run dry mid-rush. Prep in the wrong order and things peak two hours before anyone wants them.

The schedule nobody tells you

This is the solution to opening day at the kiosk. The game never shows it to you — you find it by failing, and at level eight you unlock a whiteboard to write your own version down.

The rule that makes prep matter

Freshness doesn't subtract from the score — it caps it. The worst freshness value among everything in the cup sets the ceiling. You cannot make a five-star drink out of three-hour-old pearls, no matter how perfectly you pour.

Shortage events

Every so often the distributor simply cannot get you an item for two to four days. Adapt the menu, substitute, or drop a drink. Higher account tiers get priority allocation — which is the moment supply stops being a shop menu and starts being a reason to plan ahead.

Why the late game works

At franchise stage you play one location a day. The others run on the standard operating procedures you wrote — literally your saved recipes and prep schedules, executed by staff with a skill-based error rate. Your earlier mastery becomes the content of the endgame.
